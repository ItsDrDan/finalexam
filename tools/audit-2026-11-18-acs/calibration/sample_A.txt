c1  c1: 4.00 mol

Q: Substances A and B react to form a single product, C. In each trial in the table the reaction goes until one reactant is used up. Which equation fits the data?
  FIG: {"type":"table","head":["Trial","A at start (mol)","B at start (mol)","C formed (mol)"],"rows":[["1","1.00","5.00","0.500"],["2","4.00","1.50","0.500"],["3","2.00","3.00","1.00"]]}
KEY c0: 2 A + 3 B → C
c1  c1: 3 A + 2 B → C
      why-wrong x1: These coefficients are swapped. In trial 2, find how many moles of B make 0.500 mol C.
c2  c2: 2 A + 10 B → C
      why-wrong x2: This assumes all 5.00 mol of B reacts in trial 1. Test it on trial 2, where it predicts far less C.
c3  c3: A + B → C
      why-wrong x3: With a 1 to 1 to 1 ratio, trial 1 would give 1.00 mol C. Compare that with the table.
HINT: In trial 1, B is plentiful, so A must run out. In trial 2, A is plentiful, so B must run out. Each tells you one coefficient.
EXPL: Trial 1: if A limits, 1.00 mol A gives 0.500 mol C, so 2 A per C. Trial 2: if B limits, 1.50 mol B gives 0.500 mol C, so 3 B per C. Trial 3 checks it: 2.00 mol A and 3.00 mol B are used up exactly and give 1.00 mol C. === 1A-795 | topic=advanced | d=3 | calc=0
Q: The shape of iodine pentafluoride, IF<sub>5</sub>, is drawn below, including the lone pair on iodine. What is the electron-group geometry around iodine, and what is the approximate angle between the top F and each of the four lower F atoms?
  FIG: {"type":"vsepr","shape":"square pyramidal","center":"I","lig":"F","cap":"IF₅"}
KEY c0: octahedral, just under 90°
c1  c1: trigonal bipyramidal, 90° and 120°
      why-wrong x1: That is the geometry for five electron groups. The lone pair on iodine is a sixth group.
c2  c2: square pyramidal, 109.5°
      why-wrong x2: Square pyramidal is the molecular shape, not the electron-group geometry, and 109.5° belongs to a tetrahedron.
c3  c3: octahedral, 180°
      why-wrong x3: The position 180° from the top F holds the lone pair, not a fluorine atom.
HINT: Count the electron groups on iodine, including the lone pair. Where does the lone pair push the four base fluorines?
EXPL: Iodine has 5 bonds and 1 lone pair, 6 electron groups, so the electron-group geometry is octahedral and the molecular shape is square pyramidal. The top F is at 90° to the four base F atoms, and the lone pair below squeezes them up slightly, so the angle is <b>slightly less than 90°</b> (about 82°). Trigonal bipyramidal is for 5 groups, 109.5° is the tetrahedral angle, and 180° is the angle to the lone-pair position, which holds no atom.
